Marriage, Women's Rights, and the Freedom to Choose

Published September 2026 · By Muzamil Fiaz

Honestly, I believe women deserve a lot of respect, freedom, and love. I support women's rights, especially a woman's right to make her own decisions about marriage.

Growing up and seeing life in my hometown and rural areas, I have seen how many women face problems that they cannot openly talk about. Some have no safe person to share their problems with. Many face enormous family and social pressure to get married, even when they are not ready or do not want to marry a particular person.

This made me think about marriage in a completely different way. At one point, I even started asking myself:

Why does anyone have to get married at all? Why is marriage treated as something that everyone must eventually do?

I eventually understood that marriage itself is not necessarily the problem. The problem is treating marriage as an obligation that a person cannot question.

Marriage Should Be a Choice

I strongly believe that a woman should never be forced into marriage. Her consent should matter. She should be able to say yes, no, or not yet. The same principle should apply to men as well.

Marriage is a major life decision, and it affects two people's freedom, finances, career choices, family relationships, emotional life, and future plans. It should therefore be entered into willingly by both people.

There is also a serious reason why this matters. Violence and controlling behaviour within intimate relationships are real problems. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates that around one in three women globally have experienced physical or sexual violence by an intimate partner or non-partner at some point in their lives.

What If People Choose Not to Marry?

When I say I support a woman's decision about marriage, I don't mean that every woman should choose marriage or that every woman should reject it. I mean that the decision should belong to her.

But this also raises another question:

What happens if people simply decide not to marry? Who faces more difficulties — men or women?

I don't think there is one universal answer.

Marriage can provide companionship, emotional support, shared responsibilities, financial cooperation, family life, and, for many people, children. But marriage also comes with responsibilities. You are no longer making every major decision completely on your own.

You have to consider another person's needs, safety, comfort, career, family, finances, and future plans. If children are involved, those responsibilities become even greater.

My Own Concerns About Marriage

Personally, I sometimes wonder whether I actually want those responsibilities.

I enjoy having control over my own decisions. I like being able to travel, go abroad, visit northern Pakistan, change my plans, and make major decisions without having to constantly consider another person's circumstances.

I understand that marriage can be deeply meaningful, but it can also require giving up some degree of individual freedom.

At the same time, I don't think it is fair to expect marriage to automatically provide emotional support either. A spouse is a human being, not a permanent source of emotional comfort.

Both people enter a marriage with their own needs, ambitions, insecurities, and problems. Emotional support has to be built through a healthy relationship; it cannot simply be demanded because two people are married.

Not Every Marriage Is About Money

I also have to be careful about one assumption: not every woman marries for money, and not every man marries for emotional or physical needs.

People have many different reasons for marrying — companionship, love, family, children, religious beliefs, social expectations, financial partnership, or simply the desire to build a life with someone.

The same is true in reverse. A woman may want financial security, but that does not automatically mean she is using a man. A man may want companionship, but that does not automatically mean he is genuinely ready to be a husband.

Both people can enter marriage with selfish expectations, and both can enter it with genuine intentions.

The Question We Should Really Ask

This is why I think the real question should not be:

"Why should women marry?" or "Why should men marry?"

The better question is:

"Do these two people actually want to build a life together, and are they both willing to accept the responsibilities that come with it?"

If the answer is yes, marriage can be a beautiful partnership.

If the answer is no, I don't understand why society should force them into it simply because they have reached a certain age.

The Other Side of Staying Single

Choosing not to marry also has consequences. A person who remains unmarried may have greater independence and fewer family responsibilities, but they may also have to think more carefully about companionship, ageing, illness, loneliness, financial planning, and who will support them during difficult periods of life.

These problems can affect both men and women, although the way they experience them can be very different depending on their family structure, income, culture, and social support.

So I don't think the answer is that marriage is bad or that being unmarried is better. Neither should be treated as a universal solution.

Freedom of Choice

For me, the most important principle is freedom of choice.

A woman should be free to say, "I want to marry."

She should also be free to say, "I don't want to marry."

And she should be equally free to say, "I don't want to marry this person."

The same freedom should exist for men.

I support women's rights partly because I have seen what happens when women are not given that freedom. In many communities, marriage can become less about what the woman wants and more about what her family, relatives, or society expects from her.

That can be extremely damaging when the marriage is unhappy or abusive. According to the WHO, intimate-partner violence can have serious physical, reproductive, psychological, and social consequences for women and their children.

Marriage Should Not Be a Compulsory Stage of Life

At the same time, respecting women's choices also means respecting the choices women make when they do want marriage.

Supporting women's rights should not mean telling women what their choices should be. It means giving them the freedom to make those choices themselves.

That is ultimately how I see marriage: not as a compulsory stage of life, but as a choice between two people who consciously decide that the responsibilities, compromises, companionship, and benefits are worth it for them.

If I eventually decide that I don't want that life, I should be allowed to remain unmarried without being treated as incomplete.

And if a woman decides that she wants marriage, she should be allowed to choose it without being told that she is sacrificing her independence.

The important thing is not whether someone chooses marriage or chooses to remain single.

The important thing is whether the choice is genuinely theirs.
