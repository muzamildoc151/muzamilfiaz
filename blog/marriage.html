The Future of Marriage in Pakistan: Digital Influence, Changing Norms and Rising Divorce Trends

By Muzamil Fiaz · Published in newspaper: September 2025

In Pakistan, marriage is an integral part of religious faith, cultural heritage and familial obligations. However, significant changes have gradually occurred in recent years. The concept of marriage is expected to endure through centuries; however, our perceptions regarding relationships' durability, their nature and perhaps even what they signify have undergone significant changes. Should existing patterns persist, by around the year 2050-60 it's likely that Pakistani society will see fewer stable unions formed earlier on, which tend to dissolve much sooner compared to present times.

Digital Change and Early Exposure

A crucial factor driving this transition lies in easy accessibility to smartphones. As per surveys by 2024, approximately 189 million active cellular subscriptions were in use across Pakistan's populace. The degree of accessibility significantly alters youth's modes of communication, interpersonal connections and perspectives on romantic bonds and intimate matters.

Young people establish romantic connections on digital platforms well in advance of considering marital unions. A significant number of people derive fulfillment, intimacy and camaraderie prior to reaching maturity. Research conducted at Pakistani institutions reveals that increased usage of social media diminishes familial communication hours and alters perceptions about romantic connections, personal value systems and marital commitments among adolescents. To most people, marriage is now seen as merely an additional part rather than their initial deep connection; it's more akin to adding yet another segment to a larger narrative about life events.

Rising Expectations, Lower Patience

The introduction at an early age has repercussions. Numerous studies conducted globally indicate that individuals who engage in multiple extramarital affairs typically bring into their marriages heightened hopes for deep emotional intimacy and sexual fulfillment. Concurrently, individuals exhibit reduced inclination towards maintaining conventional elements of Pakistani matrimonial practices.

The transformation can be observed within our community as well. Premarital relationships were previously deemed impractical; however, they have become increasingly prevalent in numerous cities, particularly within student populations today. Often these encounters diverge from conventional gender based expectations within marriage relationships. Consequently, numerous marriages experience disappointment as their expectations of romantic fulfillment aren't met during marital relationships often leading to feelings of dissatisfaction sooner than anticipated.

Marriage Is Happening Later and Differently

A significant alteration involves scheduling matters. Pakistani individuals particularly urban dwellers and educated ones tend to marry at an older age compared to their predecessors. Putting off getting married allows individuals ample opportunity for career advancement and self discovery; however, this delay often results in them being less flexible upon entering into matrimony.

The autonomy entails heightened demands. The perception of marriage has evolved beyond being merely an obligation for families or societal expectations into something more personally significant. Individuals seek more than merely marriage; they desire companionship characterized by emotional compatibility, intellectual alignment as well as shared interests and values. When these elevated expectations aren't met by actual outcomes, they may result in frustration and disagreement.

Divorce and Khula Are Rising

The alterations have been incorporated into these figures. Across Pakistani family courts, particularly those located in Punjab and Sindh provinces, there has been an increasing trend of filing divorce petitions and khula suits during the last twenty years. Several large urban centers such as Karachi and Lahore report substantial increases in divorce petitions.

Multiple elements contribute to it. Increased legal knowledge and economic self sufficiency for females has empowered many individuals to seek divorce in cases of unsatisfactory relationships. Perhaps more significantly than anything else, societal attitudes towards divorce seem to be changing. Previously shunned, this approach now receives more recognition for addressing problematic relationships effectively.

Marriage in 2050: A New Landscape

Should present tendencies persist into the year two thousand fifty, Pakistani marriages may bear little resemblance today:

  • Fragile partnerships often face challenges due to heightened aspirations coupled with increased prior relationships before marriage.
  • Increasing divorces may be attributed to diminishing legal, societal and cultural obstacles, particularly within metropolitan regions.
  • An evolving entity: despite maintaining core beliefs and practices, marriage's nature is set to undergo significant changes. Individuals may delay their marriages, exhibit greater autonomy in choosing partners, potentially alter the nature of marital practices by blending conventional rituals with secular ones.

Conclusion

In Pakistan, marriage remains an enduring institution yet undergoes significant transformations beyond what earlier cohorts anticipated. Modern devices like smartphones and platforms such as social media influence perceptions of romance, affectionate connections and enduring bonds in contemporary society. Individuals tend to delay marriage, seek higher standards in relationships and quickly disengage upon unmet expectations.

As of 2050, marriages in Pakistan may remain integral parts of societal life yet exhibit diminished stability, increased personal orientation and heightened vulnerability compared to current practices. The decision's impact remains uncertain; however, it is evident that such changes have begun. To safeguard our cherished principles of familial unity and communal bonds, it is imperative to comprehend these transformations and be ready for what lies in store.
